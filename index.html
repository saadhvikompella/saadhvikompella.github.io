    Kairo

    A trusted wellness assistant

    Category
    IxD, neuro-adaptive interfaces

    Tools

    01

    Overview

    Kairo is an exploratory wellness app at the intersection of neuroscience and assistive technology. It draws on audio-visual stimulation, or neural entrainment, to offer mental health support through everyday consumer technology.

    My role

    1. IdeationMusic can heal
    2. ResearchMusic and its effects, basics of neuroscience, AVS techniques and their evolution, competitor analyses
    3. UXFeature lists, use cases, edge cases, principles of usability
    4. DesignDesign system, wireframes, prototyping, audio generation

    02

    How it began

    The theory
    Music can heal. I have seen it with myself and my loved ones. As a student of music, I wanted to explore why it can have such a profound impact on the human mind.
    Validation
    To ground the theory in science, I started reading papers on music and its effects, and breaking down the parts of the brain that contribute to this journey.
    A happy accident
    During this research I came across a phenomenon called neural entrainment, and a technique built on it called audio-visual stimulation (AVS).

    03

    Research

    For weeks, I worked through research papers and videos to ground the idea in science, specifically neuroscience.

    Reading list

    • Lightening the mind with audiovisual stimulation as an accessible alternative to breath-focused meditation for mood and cognitive enhancement Johnson, Simonian & Reggente, Scientific Reports, 2024
    • A pilot study of audio-visual stimulation as a self-care treatment for insomnia in adults with insomnia and chronic pain Tang et al., Applied Psychophysiology and Biofeedback, 2014
    • The transformative power of music: Insights into neuroplasticity, health, and disease Zaatar et al., Brain, Behavior, & Immunity: Health, 2024
    25%Rise in global anxiety and depression in the first year of the pandemicWHO scientific brief, 2022
    30.5%US adults who sleep less than 7 hours a nightCDC NCHS Data Brief 559, 2024 data
    ~5 minOf AVS gave mood benefits comparable to 11 to 22 minutes of meditationJohnson et al., Scientific Reports, 2024

    04

    Insights

    Key takeaways from the research, and what they meant Kairo could do.

    1

    AVS works

    In a controlled trial, about five minutes of AVS lifted mood about as much as 11 to 22 minutes of breath-focused meditation, making it a more accessible alternative.

    2

    Music shifts cognitive states

    Music can help the mind move into a particular state through neural entrainment to certain frequencies.

    3

    Emotions can be mapped

    Emotions can be described by valence and arousal, and research links these dimensions to measurable brainwave activity, such as alpha-band patterns.

    05

    Landscape

    Existing apps are either easy to use but static, like Headspace and Calm, or adaptive but harder to use, like Endel. Kairo aims for the empty corner: adaptive and easy.

    06

    The challenge

    Area to tackle

    A consumer-friendly, adaptive mobile AVS that listens to your body and responds accordingly.

    How might we

    Establish a closed-loop system using existing, accessible and available tech ecosystems?

    07

    Features

    It is easy, and it listens to you.

    Moods

    Preset moods cover broad states of mind depending on the time of day, so you can pick the state you want to reach.

    Audio and visual

    Kairo produces soothing music with matching colour gradients that calm the mind and help reach the chosen state.

    Customization

    Create custom moods and set audio and visual styles tailored to your needs.

    08

    Closed loop

    Heart rate

    Kairo listens to your heart rate through smart wearables and translates it to your brainwave state, so the session can adapt.

    Environment

    Kairo takes note of your surroundings, factoring in ambient light, sound and the time of day.

    Suggestions

    Kairo learns from you over time and makes suggestions after each session to get the results you want.

    User journey

    An envisioned journey, built from my research and Kairo’s features rather than observed users.

    10

    Privacy

    Private by default.

    Kairo only listens to what you allow it to. Everything it stores stays private, with the sole purpose of giving you the best experience. Each signal (heart rate, environment and motion) is a separate opt-in, and every permission can be changed later.

    11

    Design system

    A sneak peek at how Kairo was made: mood tiles, home screen widgets and the app icon.

    TypefaceHost Grotesk
    Primary colour#FF9D00

    12

    Across devices

    Kairo meets you where you already are: home screen widgets on iPhone, and nudges and quick starts on Apple Watch.

    13

    Reflection

    Kairo taught me how far wearable technology has come: signals like heart rate, already collected by everyday devices, can support mood regulation without clinical equipment. The harder design problem was trust, which is why privacy became a feature rather than a footnote. Next, I would test the closed loop with real users to see whether adaptive sessions outperform static ones.

    01

    Overview

    Echo is a conversational reflective workforce system for software companies. It maps skills, capacity and live demand into a single strength graph, so the right work reaches the right person at the right time, and performance conversations stay continuous and grounded.

    1 system. Many minds.

    My role

    1. ResearchCustomer study, user interviews, market study
    2. AnalysisAffinity mapping, persona development, feature analysis
    3. DesignInformation architecture, wireframes, design system in Figma
    4. PrototypingInteractive prototype, animations, usability testing
    25People interviewed
    50+Screens designed
    4Established HR platforms compared
    2minMedian time to first assignment, down from 7 minutes

    02

    Process

    Twelve weeks, in three phases: half of the time went to research before any screen was drawn.

    1. Weeks 1–6

      Research

      Interviews, user research, journey mapping, competitor analysis and persona development.

    2. Weeks 7–10

      Ideate & create

      Low, mid and high-fidelity wireframes, the design system and the prototype.

    3. Weeks 11–12

      Testing

      Usability testing, feedback gathering and implementation.

    Observed gap

    After weeks of interviews, a common gap became clear: the distance between how people perform and what they could become.

    05

    Problems to features

    Each pattern became a problem, a root cause and a feature to answer it.

    1. ProblemFeature
    2. 01

      Employees give little importance to setting KRAs and KPIs.

      A common feeling that managers do not care about them either, “so why should they?”

      KRA/KPI alignment dashboard

      Recommends KRAs from role expectations and ties them to performance tracking, with periodic nudges to update them.

    3. 02

      Goal setting is not a priority.

      Business goals feel like enough, and personal goals feel “secondary to work”.

      Goal-setting framework with reminders

      Breaks goals into milestones inside the daily workflow, so they become routine rather than a separate burden.

    4. 03

      Appraisal cycles do not happen on time.

      Organizational issues.

      Automated appraisal scheduling

      Scheduling, reminders and escalation workflows make appraisals proactive rather than reactive.

    5. 04

      1-on-1s are rare, so earlier discussions are forgotten.

      Heavy workloads, and no immediate benefit.

      1-on-1 scheduler and tracker

      Reminders and a history of past meetings keep both sides accountable.

    6. 05

      Evaluations feel opaque.

      Poor communication about how people are assessed.

      Evaluation transparency dashboard

      A breakdown of metrics and performance factors demystifies the process and builds trust.

    7. 06

      1-on-1s go undocumented.

      Little importance is given to them.

      Meeting notes and AI summaries

      Structured notes and summaries give both people reference points for follow-ups.

    8. 07

      Heavy workloads push 1-on-1s and appraisals aside.

      Overwhelmed teams deprioritize them first, which feeds burnout.

      Workload-aware scheduling

      Suggests meeting times from workload trends, so check-ins happen when they are needed most.

    9. 08

      Resource allocation runs on spreadsheets.

      Manual, error-prone and inflexible.

      Automated resource allocation

      AI suggests allocations that spread work more evenly and reduce strain on a few people.

    06

    Landscape

    Existing management tools reduce people to spreadsheets, timelines and cold numbers. They are easy to use but static, or capable but hard to use. Echo aims to be smart and easy.

    • Feedback forms
    • Tedious
    • Rigid frameworks
    • Static
    • No evident growth

    07

    The solution

    Echo had to serve a diverse set of people, cautious and curious alike, and make clarity available to everyone, not only managers.

    1. Reflection builds self-awareness
    2. Self-awareness builds alignment
    3. Alignment builds outcomes

    Design for curious and cautious users

    One system, many minds: the same product adapts to people who explore and people who hold back.

    Design for democratizing clarity

    Transparency with hierarchy: everyone sees where they stand, within role-based access control.

    08

    Pillars

    Four pillars hold the product together.

    1

    Performance and skill tracking

    Skills, KRAs and evaluations in one place, so people know where they stand.

    2

    Task and resource allocation

    A strength graph matches work to people by skill, capacity and live demand.

    3

    Career growth

    Goals, mentors and courses that make a path forward visible.

    4

    Manager and employee collaboration

    Continuous 1-on-1s, testimonials and feedback in place of annual forms.

    09

    Ideation

    Features evolved through rounds of ideation, brainstorming and problem-solving.

    10

    Site map

    Content structuring, a site map and user flows defined how Echo’s sections connect.

    11

    Wireframes

    Rapid sketches led to low and mid-fidelity wireframes for every section. Three of them up close: the home dashboard, an AI chat and the team view.

    12

    Design system

    A monospaced wordmark gives Echo a technical voice; DM Sans keeps the interface quiet and readable.

    WordmarkECHO
    Interface typefaceDM Sans
    Brand gradient#E833EB to #3112F5

    13

    Final design

    Designed with clarity, for clarity.

    Echo tells you where you stand and supports your growth in the organization. It safeguards your information by following organizational guidelines on role-based access control.

    A smart system, and an even smarter companion.

    Echo converses with you and assists with task allocation based on the strengths and workloads of available people. With its built-in chat you can draft staffing plans, find mentors and courses, or see who is free for a coffee, and it nudges you to keep pushing yourself.

    14

    Reflection

    Echo shipped at Qentelli. I ran usability testing on prototypes across seven design iterations of more than 50 screens. Participants identified their tasks faster: improving task visibility and assignment clarity cut the median time to first assignment from 7 minutes to 2. Designing for a whole organization rather than one user taught me the importance of role-based access control in systems that everyone uses, from new hires to leadership.

    01

    Overview

    Conçu is a luxury patisserie with locations across Hyderabad and Bangalore, known for artisanal desserts and a minimal, elegant aesthetic. During my internship at Jxtapose Design Studio, I worked on its rebrand: keeping its warmth while sharpening clarity and modernity across patterns, packaging and in-store touchpoints.

    Brief

    Preserve Conçu’s warmth while making it feel clearer and more modern.

    My part

    Brand patterns for aprons, menus, packaging and coasters, grown from the heart logo.

    Principle

    Minimal but not afraid to be bold. The dessert is always the hero.

    02

    The mark

    Every pattern starts from Conçu’s infinite heart. I distilled the logo into simple geometric modules, then repeated them into tiles that echo the mark’s warmth without competing with the desserts.

    03

    Patterns

    A heart, opened like welcoming arms.

    Opening, mirroring and repeating the heart turns it into a line that reads as an invitation to share desserts, conversation and care. Generous negative space keeps the tiles breathable, echoing the café’s flow from counter to table.

    Three motif families grow from the mark: continuous line paths, tessellated “heart-argyle” grids, and rhythmic bands with diamond separators.

    04

    Palette

    A muted palette avoids loud contrasts and flatters product photography. Colours swap cleanly, so sets feel related without becoming repetitive.

    • Vanilla cream#F8F0E2
    • Cocoa#A36A58
    • Blush peach#F0D6C4
    • Sage#8AA68E
    • Powder blue#A5BDCF
    • Conçu red#BC3C38

    Low-contrast tints sit behind menus and photography, mid-contrast outlines suit boxes and sleeves, and fuller fills warm aprons and coasters.

    05

    Applications

    06

    Reflection

    The project was both personal and formative. I learned to move from concept to tangible form, balancing minimalism with approachability and aligning visual choices to business positioning. It deepened my skills as a visual designer, and it remains a project I cherish.

    01

    Overview

    I designed homepage concepts for Jxtapose Design Studio, pairing editorial text with office video and documentary stills so the firm’s work and its physical space read side by side. Split canvases, interlocking blocks and controlled overlaps create rhythm without chaos.

    Ownership

    I designed the homepage end to end, across six screens.

    Collaboration

    Built with Leaf Craft developers working across India and Australia.

    Constraint

    A palette pre-defined by the team, translated into accessible, balanced pairings.

    02

    Ideation

    A restrained black-and-white base counters visual overload and lets varied video textures sit cleanly in the foreground. Loewe and other editorial references shaped a disciplined grid with purposeful breaks.

    DisplayPP Neue Montreal
    Accent serifArapey
    Recurring ampersand&

    Six screens

    • Landing
    • Social
    • Build
    • Clients
    • Team
    • Contact

    A bottom carousel of rectangular tabs carries navigation across all six.

    03

    Process

    I prototyped several layouts per page to test visual balance, motion rhythm and technical feasibility: breakpoints, performance and file weights.

    Social

    Build

    Team

    04

    Micro-videos

    “Empty” blocks hold looping micro-videos of each team’s environment, acting as abstract mood boards that signal culture before content. I spent weeks filming the workspace on my iPhone: light grain, soft contrast and handheld movement, so the loops feel lived-in rather than staged.

    With the developers, I prepared short seamless loops with muted autoplay and tight file weights, tested 1:1 and 4:5 crops with focal-point framing, and tuned poster frames and colour so clips stayed legible on black.

    05

    Outcome

    A frame system pairing editorial text with ambient micro-videos inside a disciplined grid. Hierarchy, controlled overlap and progressive disclosure keep each page calm.

    06

    Reflection

    Working with Leaf Craft taught me to design with engineering realities: video formats, crops, file weights, breakpoints and performance budgets. The biggest lesson was balancing expressive motion with clarity. Constraints sharpened my choices and improved accessibility, load times and maintainability.

    01

    Overview

    I began learning Carnatic music at three, and today I sing and play the veena. This coffee table book is my love letter to the music: legendary composers and iconic voices sit beside the practitioners who carry it from kutcheri (concert) halls to global stages.

    Format

    An interactive coffee table book on South Indian classical music.

    Interaction

    Each section has a QR code that opens a playlist curated for its raga, composer or theme.

    Aesthetic

    Stark black and white: ink-like, meditative and uncluttered, honouring the music’s vintage soul.

    02

    Research

    The research traces a living lineage, from M.S. Subbulakshmi’s crystalline bhakti (devotion) to the Trinity who forged the music’s grammar: Thyagaraja’s ardour, Dikshitar’s architecture and Syama Sastri’s emotive depth.

    In the contemporary stream, I studied Agam’s progressive arrangements and Sid Sriram, whose early Carnatic training anchors his concert performances.

    Four ragams, mapped by mood

    • MohanaOpen, sunlit
    • ShankarabharanamBalanced, classical
    • MayamalavagowlaAustere, clear
    • KalyaniLuminous, expansive

    03

    The book

    Interactivity is organised by section. Each one opens with a scan-and-listen moment, so reading naturally becomes guided listening.

    04

    Spreads

    A gallery of influential figures threads through the book, artists who shaped the Carnatic landscape and my own ear.

    05

    A personal note

    The book closes with a reflection on growing up with the veena, the discipline of daily practice, and how singing has trained my patience, steadied my life and shaped my sense of self. It returns the project to its most intimate source.
